Most patients walk out of a consultation and forget half of what they were told. Was it one tablet or two? Can I eat before this? Is this side effect normal? The doctor doesn't have time to call everyone back, so the questions go unanswered — or get answered by a search engine.

CareLine is the call that comes the next day. It phones the patient, in Telugu, Hindi or English, and answers what they want to know about their own treatment.

## The part I cared about most

A medical assistant that always has an answer is dangerous. So CareLine has three choices for every question, not one: **answer** it if the answer is in the patient's own record and still current, **ask** a clarifying question if it's unclear, or **hand off** to the doctor if it isn't sure. Saying "let me check with your doctor" is treated as a success, not a failure.

<CaseCallout label="the design rule" claim="Trust is the product.">
A patient will only pick up the second call if the first one was right. So the safety checks sit underneath the AI, not inside it — and no new version goes out until it passes a set of real, labelled patient questions.
</CaseCallout>

## Where it is

It works end to end: the call, the conversation, the hand-off to the doctor. The next step is a pilot with a real clinic — consent, real recordings, and real patients.
